## 1. Gap Statement & Context  

Safety‑awareness training is recognised as a cornerstone of workshop practice, yet its **direct impact on instructors’ capacity to teach psychomotor competencies in construction‑technology laboratories has not been quantified**. Existing research on the topic is fragmented:  
- One strand investigates **teacher competency in practical construction‑technology instruction** without linking it to safety‑training outcomes [@Kompetensi guru dalam pengajaran amali teknologi pembinaan di kolej vokasional].  
- Another strand explores **factors influencing safety awareness among workshop instructors** but does not examine how this awareness translates into teaching effectiveness for skill‑intensive tasks [@Influencing Factors towards Safety Awareness among Instructors in Selected Public TVET Institutions].  

Consequently, **the literature lacks an integrated assessment of whether, and how, safety‑awareness training enhances instructors’ ability to deliver psychomotor skill instruction**. Addressing this gap can inform curriculum design, instructor professional development, and ultimately improve both student learning and workplace safety in construction‑technology labs.

## 3. Methodological Recommendations  

To bridge the identified gap, future investigations should adopt a **mixed‑methods, quasi‑experimental design** that simultaneously captures changes in safety awareness and teaching performance:  

1. **Pre‑/post safety‑awareness training intervention** – deliver a standardized safety‑awareness module to a cohort of construction‑technology instructors and compare outcomes with a control group.  
2. **Quantitative safety‑awareness measurement** – use validated safety‑culture questionnaires (e.g., adapted from instruments used in prior safety‑awareness studies) to assess attitudinal change [@Influencing Factors towards Safety Awareness among Instructors in Selected Public TVET Institutions].  
3. **Objective teaching‑performance assessment** – develop a rubric for psychomotor skill delivery (e.g., demonstration fidelity, student error rates) building on the competency dimensions identified in teacher‑competency research [@Kompetensi guru dalam pengajaran amali teknologi pembinaan di kolej vokasional].  
4. **Classroom/ laboratory observations** – conduct structured observations of instructors during lab sessions to capture behavioural indicators of safety integration and instructional quality.  
5. **Qualitative interviews or focus groups** – explore instructors’ perceptions of how safety training influences their instructional strategies, complementing the quantitative data.  

Statistical analyses (e.g., ANCOVA controlling for baseline competency) can determine whether safety‑awareness training yields **significant improvements in the delivery of psychomotor tasks**, while qualitative insights can elucidate underlying mechanisms.
